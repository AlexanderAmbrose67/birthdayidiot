AN EMBER

There was an ember buried beneath the grey,

Left there by a fire that had burned itself away.

The sky had gone black, the earth had turned cold,

And still that little ember refused to do what it was told.

The wind came first, with its teeth and its lies,

It scattered the ash and darkened the skies.

It whispered, Go colder. It’s easier that way.

Let the heat disappear with the end of the day.

No flame means no burning. No warmth means no pain.

No wanting, no hoping, no starting again.

It had every reason to smother its flame,

To curse every warmth that had ever brought pain.

It hated its softness, its heat, and its glow,

As though being gentle were something to owe.

So it tried to go cold, as the winter had taught,

To bury its warmth and extinguish each thought.

But coldness is easy, the dead learn it fast,

It takes no courage to live in the past.

The ember had watched all the forests decay,

Had learned that the cruelest things sometimes stay.

It watched softer things break beneath heavier skies,

Then wondered why softness still lived in its eyes.

It mistook every tear for a weakness to hide,

Every trembling breath for a reason to die.

It called itself fragile whenever it bent,

Never noticing how much it had already spent.

It thought being colder might finally be wise,

might make it less breakable, easier to survive.

For ash never trembles, and stone never cries,

and nothing can hurt what has already died.

For stone does not choose whether stone it will be,

Nor does winter decide whether winter must freeze.

But something still living, when given the choice,

Can harden its edges... or still keep its voice.

For heat doesn't vanish because darkness demands,

and softness is not the same thing as weak hands.

A thing can be gentle and still refuse chains,

can survive being buried beneath other things' blame.

The ember knew nothing of victory or pride,

it only knew something was burning inside.

No thunder announced it. No heavens took note.

It simply kept breathing beneath what would choke.

And perhaps there is courage in refusing to cease,

In remaining still tender when tenderness costs peace.

The world may keep trying to bury its glow,

And perhaps it will hurt more than anyone knows.

Perhaps there will be winters it cannot outrun,

And mornings when keeping alight feels undone.

But perhaps that is why it was harder to break,

it never mistook being gentle for being a mistake.

It never grew cruel just because cruelty came,

nor turned all its tenderness into something to blame.

So if someday the ember forgets what it's worth,

If it thinks it was foolish for staying on earth,

Let someone remind it, when everything ends,

That surviving the dark is not something to mend.

And years from now, when the old stones are gone,

when the night has forgotten what once burned at dawn,

perhaps someone passing will notice the glow,

and wonder how anything buried could show.

So if the world tells it that softness should cease,

that numbness is safer, that coldness is peace,

let the world keep talking. Let winter persist.

Some things are worth keeping precisely like this.

For this life was never a promise of peace,

nor that every sorrow would someday cease.

Some things we endure, not because they are fair,

but because there is mercy beyond what is here.

And perhaps when the test and the long nights are done,

the ember will rest somewhere warmer than sun,

where softness is strength, and no hurt can remain,

and an ember may rest without fearing the rain.

And if it forgets, when the night becomes long,

If it starts believing that cold would be strong,

Let somebody whisper, when nothing else will,

You were never weak. You were warm. You are still.

So happy birthday, ember. Try not to burn through the year.

Try not to collect any more reasons to disappear.

And if the world grows colder and buries your blaze,

Keep one spark beneath the ash for another few days.

You don't have to become fire. You don't have to be bright.

You don't have to prove you deserve to survive the night.

And if you forget that, as you inevitably may,

I'll remind you. Annoyingly. Until you believe what I say.
